Research Focus

I lead the design, architecture, and evaluation of large multimodal models and world models for visual reasoning in documents, images, and video. My work spans recovering structure from complex document layouts, grounding language in objects and image regions, and tracking motion and identity across frames. I design representations and training objectives for these tasks, then test whether specific visual evidence supports each model’s answers.

Selected work

Research Highlights

Object structure

Preserve scene coordinates when encoding individual regions. Use geometry and connectivity to learn how parts fit together, then read out boxes, masks, and internal structure from the same representation.

Identity and change in video

Combine appearance, pose, and motion across frames to track and retrieve the same person within a video. For authenticity checks, compare a video predictor’s expected representation with the observed one to detect signs of manipulation.

Visual memory and grounding

Let a language decoder revisit stored regions to locate, count, or segment objects. Route box predictions through the selected visual evidence and test whether substituting mismatched regions changes the result.

Document extraction

Link field-level errors to a schema’s descriptions and grouping. Test combinations of edits with the extractor fixed, preserving output keys and types so improvements can be traced to the schema.

Policy reasoning

Encode conditions, scope, and dependencies as a graph. Change individual constraints, check whether the decision changes as required, and trace where the verifier first loses the distinction.
